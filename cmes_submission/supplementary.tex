\section{Contrast-solve truncation sweep}

The contrast step uses a fixed $n_{\mathrm{cg}}=\numv{\NumNCg}$-step conjugate-gradient loop.
The certified blocks $C$ and $D$ are closed-form and touch no iterative solve; the contrast-path
blocks $A$, $B$ and the full-layer norm that contains them do.

With the implemented $n_{\mathrm{cg}}$ the relative residual of the regularised system is
$\numv{\NumCgResidImpl}$; it falls to $\numv{\NumCgResidConv}$ at
$n_{\mathrm{cg}}=\numv{\NumNCgConv}$. The field block $D$ contains no iterative solve, and there
the analytic directional derivative and a central difference agree to
$\numv{\NumFdBlockD}$. On the contrast-path block $A$ at the implemented truncation the central
difference and the analytic directional derivative disagree by $\numv{\NumFdBlockAImpl}$; the
relative discrepancy does not shrink as the finite-difference step is refined, which is the
signature of a difference quotient that does not converge to the derivative at that truncation.
Converging the solve restores the agreement to $\numv{\NumFdBlockAConv}$.

The full-layer norm mixes the two CG-free blocks with the two solver-dependent ones, so it
inherits the artefact even though its own closure has no solver in it. It is reported because a
reader who wants one number per layer will reach for it, and it is never certified: a statement
about $\sigma_{\max}(DT_k)$ would be a statement about the truncation the trained weights happen
to be calibrated to.
